Oil Flag Breakout Rules

The complete rule set, written so every decision is mechanical. After a strong move in crude, a tight pause forms; buy or sell the break of that pause in the direction of the move.

Every rule below is the rule that was backtested (see the research report). Changing any of them means trading an untested system.

Terms used below

Definitions

Daily barOne CME trading session, 18:00 to 17:00 New York time. The bar is dated by the day it closes. Sunday evening trading belongs to Monday's bar.
True rangeThe largest of: high − low, |high − previous close|, |low − previous close|.
ATRSimple average of the last 20 true ranges, in price units ($ per barrel).
Impulse window4, 5 or 6 consecutive daily bars. Its start close is the close of the bar just before the window.
Impulse size (I)Highest price minus lowest price across the window, counting the start close.
Impulse extreme (P1)The window's highest price after an up-impulse, lowest after a down-impulse.
BoxThe 4, 5 or 6 bars right after the impulse window. Box high = their highest high, box low = their lowest low.
Setup dayThe last bar of the box. Everything is checked at its close.
TickMinimum price step: $0.01 for CL, MCL and BZ.
Checked at the close of the setup day

Setup

  1. Impulse. Over a window of 4, 5 or 6 bars, the last close minus the start close is at least 1.5 × ATR, measured on the bar just before the window. The sign gives the direction.
    |close(last bar) − start close| ≥ 1.5 × ATR(bar before window)
  2. Box. The next 4, 5 or 6 bars form the box. Its height must be at most half the impulse size.
    box high − box low ≤ 0.5 × I
  3. Hold most of the move. After an up-impulse, the box low stays above P1 − 0.618 × I. After a down-impulse, the box high stays below P1 + 0.618 × I.
  4. Volatility filter. On the setup day, ATR ÷ close is higher than on at least half of the previous 251 trading days. If not, there is no trade.
  5. No overlap. An impulse window must start after the last setup day found with the same window and box lengths. You never reuse part of an old box as a new impulse.
  6. All nine combinations count. Check every pair of impulse length (4, 5, 6) and box length (4, 5, 6). Usually several describe the same box. If they give different boxes, place orders for each; the first one that fills is the trade (R13).
Placed before the next session opens

Orders and size

  1. Entry. After an up-impulse, a buy stop 1 tick above the box high. After a down-impulse, a sell stop 1 tick below the box low. If the market gaps through the level, the fill at the open counts.
  2. Order life. The entry order is good for the next 10 trading days. If it has not filled by the close of day 10, cancel it.
  3. Cancel on the other side. If price trades at or beyond the opposite edge of the box before the entry fills, cancel the entry. That is the box low for a long, the box high for a short.
  4. Protective stop. The opposite edge of the box, but never closer than 0.5 × ATR (the ATR from R1) to the entry level. Place it as a resting stop order the moment the entry fills.
    long stop = min(box low, box high − 0.5 × ATR) short stop = max(box high, box low + 0.5 × ATR)
  5. Position size. Size from the planned entry level to the stop, and round down to an even number of contracts so the position splits in half. If even 2 of the smallest contract risk too much, skip the trade.
    contracts = (account × risk %) ÷ (|entry level − stop| × units per contract) units: CL 1,000 · MCL 100 · BZ 1,000 · CFD per your broker
Two halves, managed separately

Exits

  1. Half 1, measured move. Limit order at the entry level plus one impulse size (minus for shorts). Its stop stays at the R10 stop. If neither is hit, close it at the close of the 20th trading day after entry.
    target = entry level ± I
  2. Half 2, trailing runner. After every daily close, starting with the entry day, compute close − 3.5 × ATR for a long (close + 3.5 × ATR for a short). If that is tighter than the current stop, move the stop there. Never loosen it. Close this half at the close of the 60th trading day if it is still open.
  3. No other exits. No moving stops to break-even, no early profit-taking, no exits on news. The average trade depends on the few runners that go far.
Across positions

Portfolio rules

  1. One position per market. While a trade is open, ignore new setups in that market. Once both halves are closed, any setup whose entry order is still within its 10 days can be taken.
  2. WTI and Brent are one bet. They move together. If both signal the same way within a few days, either trade one of them or split the risk (0.5% each at a 1% budget).
  3. Futures rolls. Find setups on a back-adjusted continuous chart. Place orders in the contract you trade, shifted by the roll difference if the box spans a roll. Roll open positions before first notice and keep the stop and target the same distance from price. CFD prices are already continuous.
  4. Take every signal. The three best trades produced about half of all profit in the backtests. Skipping signals because they "look wrong" removes the edge.
About 10 minutes after the 17:00 New York close

Daily routine

  1. Update the chart and ATR(20). Or run python3 scan.py --csv your_daily.csv, which checks R1–R6 and prints open positions and live orders with their levels.
  2. Manage open trades first: move half 2's trailing stop (R13), check the 20- and 60-day time stops (R12, R13).
  3. Look for a new setup if flat: an impulse (R1), a box (R2, R3), the filter (R4).
  4. Write the order ticket: entry stop, cancel level, protective stop, target, size, last valid day.
  5. Place the orders before 18:00 New York: the entry stop, and bracket orders that attach the stop and target on fill if your platform supports them.
  6. Check pending entries: cancel any that reached day 10 or whose opposite box edge traded.
  7. Log it: every setup, taken or not, with the levels and the result in R.

Worked example

Order ticket · WTI · longAccount $50,000 · risk 1%
IMPULSE5 bars, 8–14 January. Start close $56.01 (7 January), last close $61.84: +$5.83. ATR on 7 January was $1.41, so the threshold was 1.5 × 1.41 = $2.12. Pass.
BOX5 bars, 15–22 January (19 January was a holiday). High $60.38, low $59.13, height $1.25, limit 0.5 × 5.83 = $2.92. Hold line 61.84 − 0.618 × 5.83 = $58.24; box low $59.13 is above it. Pass.
FILTERATR ÷ close on 22 January ranked above 70% of the past year. Pass.
ENTRYBuy stop $60.39 (box high + 1 tick), good through 5 February. Cancel if $59.13 trades first.
STOP$59.13 (box low; the 0.5 × ATR floor would be $59.67, so the box low is the wider of the two).
SIZE$500 ÷ ($1.25 × 100 barrels) = 4 MCL contracts: 2 for the target, 2 for the runner.
HALF 1Target $60.38 + $5.83 = $66.21, time stop 20 trading days.
HALF 2Trail 3.5 × ATR behind each close, time stop 60 trading days.
RESULTIn the closing-price backtest the target was reached on 19 February. The runner rode the March supply shock and was stopped on 17 April at $85.90. Net +12.4R.

The example uses daily closes, so its highs and lows are closes too. On a normal OHLC chart the box and levels come out slightly different.

Start small

  • First 10 trades: paper, or 0.5% risk.
  • Compare every 10 trades with the table above.
  • Move to 1% only after the live numbers look like the backtest.

Stop and review if

  • The drawdown from the equity peak reaches 15R, beyond the worst seen.
  • 8 losses come in a row, against a historical worst of 5.
  • After 30 trades the average is below zero.
Tested and rejected

Never

  1. Don't trade against the impulse. Box breaks against the move, failed-breakout reversals, fading the spike and range trades inside the box all failed out of sample.
  2. Don't apply the rules to intraday charts. On 1-hour and 4-hour bars the same rules fail after costs.
  3. Don't trade natural gas with it. Every variant lost in at least one decade.
  4. Don't add filters on the fly. A trend filter was tested and was not reliable. New filters, targets or stop tweaks need their own backtest first.
Before real money: rerun the backtest on your broker's own continuous futures or CFD history with scan.py. The realistic 1-minute test covers only WTI up to 2020 (34 trades). Everything after that relies on daily closing prices.
