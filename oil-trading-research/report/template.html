Oil & gas bot research

We backtested 11 strategy families on 33 years of roll-adjusted futures, 15 years of one-minute CFD prices and EIA spot prices up to 22 Sep 2026, charging retail CFD spreads and swap costs. Trend following is the one durable edge, and a refining-margin (crack spread) signal makes it better for crude. Most of the ideas people usually build into oil bots lost money.

What is worth building

Net Sharpe ratio after CFD spreads, 2.5%/yr financing markup and roll yield. Daily strategies are scored on the equal-weight portfolio of the markets they trade, from 1991 to March 2024; out-of-sample means 2008 onwards, with parameters chosen on the earlier years. For a sense of scale, a Sharpe of 0.5 over 33 years is a solid systematic strategy, and anything above 1.5 in a daily oil backtest should make you look for a bug.

Direction 1 · core

A daily trend bot with a crack-spread tilt

This is the configuration we would build first. It trades all three CFDs once a day (or once a week), holds positions for weeks to months, and sizes every market to the same risk. The backtest below is net of costs.

Markets
XTIUSD, XBRUSD, XNGUSD, equal risk. Gas trades trend only; crude trades 50% trend and 50% crack signal.
Trend signal
Average of four EWMA crossovers (8/32, 16/64, 32/128, 64/256 days) and four breakout rules (40, 80, 160, 320 days), each scaled by volatility. Computed on a roll-adjusted daily close, never on the raw CFD chart if it has roll gaps.
Crack signal
z-score of the 3-2-1 crack spread (2×RBOB + 1×heating oil/ULSD − 3×WTI, $/bbl) against its own 250-day history. Long crude when refining margins are high, short when they are low. Needs a daily feed of RBOB and ULSD settlements (CME RB, HO).
Position size
Target 15% annualised volatility per market: position = forecast × 15% ÷ forecast volatility, using a 36-day EWMA blended 70/30 with the 10-year average. Typical exposure is 0.3–0.6× equity per market; cap 3×.
Trading
Rebalance daily or weekly (Wednesday gave the same result). Execute in the 30 minutes before the 14:30 ET settlement, when CFD spreads are tightest. Skip trades smaller than 10% of the typical position.
Risk overlay
Halve a market's exposure when its volatility is above the 90th percentile of the past five years. Trend lost money in that regime in all three markets. Portfolio kill-switch at −25% from peak for manual review; the backtest's worst drawdown was −24%.
Costs assumed
Per-side spread + slippage of 3 bp (WTI), 3.5 bp (Brent), 10 bp (gas), plus a 2.5%/yr financing markup on both longs and shorts. Doubling or tripling spreads barely matters; a 5% markup takes about 0.09 off the Sharpe ratio.
Check this with your broker before writing code. The futures returns we use include roll yield: backwardation pays longs, contango charges them. A CFD passes that through only if its overnight swap is set from the futures curve. Ask how XTIUSD, XBRUSD and XNGUSD are priced, how the monthly roll is handled, and what the long and short swaps are. If the swap is a flat interest-style rate, the same trend rules applied to spot-style prices earned roughly nothing in our tests (Sharpe −0.1 to 0.1 for crude).
Evidence · trend following

Trend works in all three markets and in every decade

Every trend rule we tried made money on the rolled futures after costs, from simple 12-month momentum to breakouts and moving-average crossovers. Blends of several speeds were the most stable. The edge is modest per market (Sharpe 0.4–0.5) and improves when the three markets are combined, because oil and gas trends are only loosely related.

Robust to the usual stress tests

Trading one or two days late changes the Sharpe by 0.02. Doubling or tripling spreads costs about 0.03. With no position buffer, or a 25% buffer, it moves by ±0.04. Every one of 26 trend variants was positive.

Mostly small losses, a few big wins

About three direction changes a year per market, held 1–3 months. Roughly four in five direction changes lose around 2% of equity; the trends that run gain 20–28%. Both long and short sides contribute in each market.

Part of the edge is roll yield

The same positions applied to EIA spot returns earned a Sharpe of −0.1 to 0.1 for crude, against 0.4–0.5 on futures. Trend tends to be long in backwardation and short in contango, so it collects carry. That is why the broker's swap method matters.

Evidence · refining margin

The crack spread predicts crude, and not only through trend

When gasoline and diesel are expensive relative to crude compared with the past year, refiners buy more crude and product-led rallies follow. A position proportional to the 250-day z-score of the 3-2-1 crack spread earned a net Sharpe of 0.61 on WTI and Brent together (0.44 before 2008, 0.76 after). Its daily P&L is 0.51 correlated with trend, so combining the two lifts the three-market portfolio from 0.58 to 0.69 and cuts the worst drawdown from −32% to −24%.

Weak points: most of the signal comes from gasoline (Sharpe 0.60 alone vs 0.33 for heating oil). On its own, the crack signal built from near-month WTI instead of the December contract scored 0.41 for 2008–2024 rather than 0.76; inside the trend + crack blend the near-month version still reached 0.66. It survives seasonal adjustment (0.54) and doubled costs (0.58). We could not test it after March 2024 because the product prices in our data stop there. Paper-trade it before giving it full weight.

Evidence · carry and curve shape

Carry looks strong until you trade it a day later

Going long when the futures curve is in backwardation and short in contango scored 0.50 on the three-market portfolio with same-close execution. With a one-day delay it fell to 0.35, and for WTI the 2008–2024 result went from 0.53 to 0.07. Much of the raw signal was day-to-day noise in the spread between contracts. A 60-day smoothed version was robust to delay but weak after 2008 (0.35). Used as a filter on trend it added 0.02. Treat carry as a cost to track, not a signal to trade.

Evidence · what failed

Mean reversion, spreads and ratios lose money

Oil does not revert at daily-to-monthly horizons; it trends. All 22 daily reversal and fade rules lost money on the three-market portfolio after costs, most of them before costs too. The one exception, buying two-day RSI dips inside an uptrend, is a trend strategy in disguise and was weak after 2007 (0.17).

The Brent–WTI timing trap

On EIA spot prices, fading the Brent–WTI spread showed Sharpe ratios of 2.5–3.1. That is an artefact: Brent is assessed in London hours before WTI settles in New York, so the spread "reverts" the next morning. Trading one day later drops it to 0.2–0.5. On synchronous futures prices the same rules lost money (−0.8 to −2.0). Any spread bot must compare prices taken at the same moment.

Oil/gas ratio

Betting that the WTI/gas price ratio returns to normal lost money (−0.2 to −0.5). The ratio moved from about 10 to over 40 after the shale gas boom and never came back. Structural breaks make these ratio trades dangerous.

Out-of-sample · Apr 2024 – Sep 2026

The last 30 months were bad for trend

Our futures data ends in March 2024, so we ran the trend rule on EIA spot prices for the period since. Spot understates a futures-based result because it misses roll yield, but year by year the two tracked each other closely from 2010 to 2024. Trend lost money: WTI −0.74 and Brent −0.77 Sharpe. The 2024–25 range around $60–80 produced repeated false starts. In March 2026 the rule was long into the spike and made 13% (WTI) and 20% (Brent) that month. It then gave most of that back in the May–July swings.

What this means for the bot

Losing stretches of two to five years are normal for trend strategies: in the backtest there was a 30% chance of a losing year and the longest time under water was 4.8 years. The current market is in the regime where trend has done worst, with Brent volatility in its top decile. That argues for starting small, paper trading first and keeping the volatility overlay switched on.

Method, data and caveats

How the tests were run

Data

  • Roll-adjusted futures from the pysystemtrade repository: WTI (December contract), Brent (~2nd month), Henry Hub gas (~2nd month), RBOB, heating oil; 1989 – 28 Mar 2024. Near-month WTI and gas series (2006+) used as checks.
  • EIA daily spot prices for WTI, Brent and Henry Hub, 1986 – 22 Sep 2026.
  • Oanda one-minute mid prices for the WTI and natural gas CFDs, Jan 2005 – May 2020. There was no intraday Brent.
  • Yahoo, FRED, EIA's API, Dukascopy, Stooq and CFTC were blocked in this sandbox, so no COT positioning, inventory surprises or futures after March 2024.

Rules

  • Signals use only data up to the close; trades fill at that close and earn from the next day. We repeated key tests with 1–10 day delays.
  • Parameters were picked on 1990–2007 and judged on 2008–2024; spot data after March 2024 is an untouched holdout.
  • Every market is sized to 15% annual volatility, so results are comparable across gas and crude.
  • We ran about 130 portfolio-level variants. The recommended strategies have t-statistics of 3.4–4.0 over 33 years, above the t > 3 bar suggested for new strategies. The expected best Sharpe from 130 random strategies would be about 0.5.

Where the backtest can mislead

  • CFD swaps are modelled as futures roll yield plus a 2.5% markup; your broker may differ.
  • The main WTI series holds the December contract. On near-month contracts, which are closer to what the CFDs track, 2008–2024 Sharpe was 0.45 for trend and 0.66 for trend + crack, against 0.54 and 0.76 on the main series.
  • Henry Hub spot behaves very differently from gas futures. The XNGUSD CFD follows futures, so ignore spot-based gas results.
  • Weekend gaps, broker outages and the April 2020 negative-price event are not fully captured by daily closes.
