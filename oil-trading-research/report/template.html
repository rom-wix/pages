Oil & gas bot research

What would a trading bot on these three CFDs have done over the last 12 months, and which rules hold up over 33 years of data? We tested 11 strategy families with retail CFD spreads and swap costs. Trend following is the one durable edge, and in this year's headline-driven market only its faster versions paid. A refining-margin (crack spread) signal improves it for crude. Most ideas people usually build into oil bots lost money.

Last 12 months · 23 Sep 2025 – 22 Sep 2026 · EIA daily prices

A shock year that paid fast trend rules and punished slow ones

Crude spent the autumn drifting down to $55–60, jumped 75–90% between mid-February and early April 2026, fell back to about $71 by late June, and rallied again into September. Brent was more volatile than in any calendar year since 1990 except 2020. A handful of days did most of the damage or the paying: the ten biggest days made up 35–42% of the year's variance.

What worked

Fast trend rules with one- to three-month memory: 1-month momentum scored 1.38 and a 20-day Donchian breakout 1.30 on crude. The recommended blend made +9.7% on WTI and +5.4% on Brent (Sharpe 0.74 and 0.48) with drawdowns under 10%. Holding long also worked (1.36), which is hindsight after a 50–70% rally.

What did not

Slow trend rules were short going into the March spike and whipsawed after it: 160–320 day breakouts scored −0.8 to −1.8. Mean reversion lost again (median rule −0.68). A permanent short scored −1.47. The original, slower trend blend was flat to slightly negative.

What we could not test yet

Intraday rules, the crack spread and carry need data we don't have for this period: broker minute bars, RBOB and ULSD prices, and the futures curve. Your market-data server would cover the first, but this sandbox's network policy blocks it. Once it's allowed, the intraday section can be rerun on the last year.

Direction 1 · core

A multi-speed trend bot with a crack-spread tilt

This is the configuration we would build first. It trades all three CFDs once a day (or once a week), holds positions for weeks to months, and sizes every market to the same risk. It mixes fast and slow trend components: a slow-only version was flat in the last year, and the mix was at least as good over 33 years.

Markets
XTIUSD, XBRUSD and XNGUSD at equal risk. Gas trades trend only; crude trades 50% trend and 50% crack signal.
Trend signal
Average of two halves. Half is 1-, 3- and 12-month momentum: the sign of the return over each window, averaged (Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen 2017). The other half is four EWMA crossovers (4/16, 8/32, 16/64, 32/128 days) and four breakout rules (20, 40, 80, 160 days), each scaled by volatility. Compute everything on roll-adjusted daily closes, never on a raw CFD chart with roll gaps.
Crack signal
z-score of the 3-2-1 crack spread (2×RBOB + 1×heating oil/ULSD − 3×WTI, in $/bbl) against its own 250-day history. Go long crude when refining margins are high and short when they are low. This needs a daily feed of RBOB and ULSD settlements (CME RB and HO).
Position size
Target 15% annualised volatility per market: position = forecast × 15% ÷ forecast volatility, using a 36-day EWMA blended 70/30 with the 10-year average. Typical exposure is 0.3–0.6× equity per market; cap 3×.
Volatility overlay
Halve the trend part when a market's volatility is above the 90th percentile of its past five years. Brent has been in that zone for much of 2026.
Trading
Rebalance daily or weekly (Wednesday gave the same result). Execute in the 30 minutes before the 14:30 ET settlement, when CFD spreads are tightest. Skip trades smaller than 10% of the typical position.
Risk limits
Portfolio kill-switch at −20% from peak for manual review; the backtest's worst drawdown was −19%. Keep at least 50% free margin, and don't add to gas longs into Thursday's storage report: gas fell 26 bp on report days on average.
Costs assumed
Per-side spread + slippage of 3 bp (WTI), 3.5 bp (Brent) and 10 bp (gas), plus a 2.5%/yr financing markup on longs and shorts. Financing is the bigger cost for crude: about 1.0–1.1% of equity a year per market, against 0.3% for spreads. Gas pays about 0.7% for each.
Check this with your broker before writing code. The futures returns we use include roll yield: backwardation pays longs and contango charges them. A CFD passes that through only if its overnight swap is set from the futures curve. Ask how XTIUSD, XBRUSD and XNGUSD are priced, how the monthly roll is handled, and what the long and short swaps are. If the swap is a flat interest-style rate, the same trend rules applied to spot-style prices earned roughly nothing over the long run (Sharpe −0.05 for WTI and 0.07 for Brent).
Evidence · trend following

Trend works in all three markets and in every decade

Every one of 24 trend rules we tried made money on the rolled futures after costs, from simple momentum to breakouts and moving-average crossovers. Blends of several speeds were the most stable. The edge is modest per market (Sharpe 0.4–0.5) and improves when the three markets are combined, because oil and gas trends are only loosely related (daily P&L correlation about 0.08).

Robust to the usual stress tests

For the recommended bot, trading one day late costs about 0.06 Sharpe and four days late about 0.18; its fast components care more about timing than the slow blend does. Doubling spreads costs 0.04, tripling them 0.08, and a 5% financing markup 0.09. On near-month contracts, closer to what the CFDs track, 2008–2024 results were about 0.1 lower.

Mostly small losses, a few big wins

About three direction changes a year per market, each held for one to three months. Roughly four in five direction changes lose around 2% of equity. The trends that run gain 20–28%. Both long and short sides contribute in every market.

Part of the edge is roll yield

The same positions applied to EIA spot returns earned −0.05 (WTI) and 0.07 (Brent) over the long run, against 0.39 and 0.49 on futures. Trend tends to be long in backwardation and short in contango, so it collects carry. That is why the broker's swap method matters.

Evidence · refining margin

The crack spread predicts crude, and not only through trend

When gasoline and diesel are expensive relative to crude compared with the past year, refiners buy more crude and product-led rallies follow. A position proportional to the 250-day z-score of the 3-2-1 crack spread earned a net Sharpe of 0.61 on WTI and Brent together: 0.44 before 2008 and 0.76 after. Its daily P&L is 0.51 correlated with trend. Adding it lifts the recommended bot from 0.69 to 0.79 and cuts the worst drawdown from −23% to −19%.

Weak points: most of the signal comes from gasoline (0.60 alone against 0.33 for heating oil). Built from near-month WTI instead of the December contract, the signal on its own scored 0.41 for 2008–2024 rather than 0.76. Inside the blend, the near-month version still reached 0.66. It survives seasonal adjustment (0.54) and doubled costs (0.58). We could not test it after March 2024 because the product prices in our data stop there. Paper-trade it before giving it full weight.

Evidence · carry and curve shape

Carry looks strong until you trade it a day later

Going long when the futures curve is in backwardation and short in contango scored 0.50 on the three-market portfolio when traded at the signal's own close. With a one-day delay it fell to 0.35. For the scaled WTI version, the 2008–2024 result went from 0.53 to 0.07. Much of the raw signal is day-to-day noise in the spread between contracts. A 60-day smoothed version was robust to delay but weak after 2008 (0.35). As a filter on trend it added about 0.01. Treat carry as a cost to track, not a signal to trade.

Evidence · what failed

Mean reversion, spreads and ratios lose money

Oil does not revert at daily-to-monthly horizons; it trends. Of 19 short-horizon reversal and shock rules, 18 lost money on the three-market portfolio after costs, most of them even before costs. The one exception, buying two-day RSI dips inside an uptrend, is a trend strategy in disguise and was weak after 2007 (0.17). The last 12 months said the same (median rule −0.68).

The Brent–WTI timing trap

On EIA spot prices, fading the Brent–WTI spread showed Sharpe ratios of 2.5–3.1. That is an artefact: Brent is assessed in London hours before WTI settles in New York, so the spread "reverts" the next morning. Trading one day later drops it to 0.2–0.5 (0.55 in the last year). On synchronous futures prices the same rules lost money (−0.8 to −2.0). Any spread bot must compare prices taken at the same moment.

Oil/gas ratio

Betting that the WTI/gas price ratio returns to normal lost money (−0.2 to −0.5). The ratio went from about 10 to over 40 after the shale gas boom and never came back. Structural breaks make these ratio trades dangerous.

Evidence · calendar

No calendar bot, one small crude tilt

No month-of-year effect was statistically significant on the futures, and monthly patterns did not repeat between 1990–2007 and 2008–2024 (correlation 0.06–0.27). Walk-forward seasonal rules that re-estimate the pattern from the previous 5–15 years all lost money. Across 119 calendar variants, luck alone would produce a best Sharpe of about 0.75; the best we found was 0.56.

Why the gas seasonal fails

Henry Hub spot does rise into October and fall in February, but the futures curve already prices the winter premium. The held contract's annualised carry is −33% in August, −79% in September and −62% in October, exactly when an autumn long is held. What spot gains, a futures-based CFD pays back through the roll.

Gas storage-report days

Gas futures fell 26 bp on average on EIA storage-report days (usually Thursday), against +2 bp on other days. It is the one calendar effect that survives multiple-testing correction (t = −3.4), but at 10 bp a side it can't be traded. Use it as a risk rule instead.

The crude tilt, honestly

The driving-season rule is positive in both halves and cheap to run, but its exact months were partly chosen by looking, and it is not significant after correction. It had a great last 12 months (1.37) because it was long through the spring spike. If you use it, keep it small.

Out-of-sample · Apr 2024 – Sep 2026

Since our futures data ends, trend has struggled

Our futures data ends in March 2024, so for the 30 months since we ran the trend rules on EIA spot prices. Spot understates a futures-based result because it misses roll yield, but year by year the two tracked each other closely from 2010 to 2024. The recommended blend lost modestly (WTI −0.35, Brent −0.52), almost all of it in the 2024–25 range between $60 and $80. The original slow blend lost more (−0.74 and −0.77).

What this means for the bot

Losing stretches of two to four years are normal for trend strategies. In the backtest there was a 30% chance of a losing year, and the longest time under water was 3.9 years. The market is in the regime where trend has done worst historically, with Brent volatility in its top decile. That argues for starting small, paper trading first and keeping the volatility overlay switched on.

Method, data and caveats

How the tests were run

Data

  • Roll-adjusted futures from the pysystemtrade repository: WTI (December contract), Brent (~2nd month), Henry Hub gas (~2nd month), RBOB and heating oil; 1989 – 28 Mar 2024. Near-month WTI and gas series from 2006 were used as checks.
  • EIA daily spot prices for WTI, Brent and Henry Hub, 1986 – 22 Sep 2026. These are the only source for the last 12 months.
  • Oanda one-minute mid prices for the WTI and natural-gas CFDs, Jan 2005 – May 2020. There was no intraday Brent.
  • The sandbox blocked Yahoo, FRED, the EIA API, Dukascopy, Stooq, CFTC and your market-data server. So there is no COT positioning, no inventory surprises, and no futures or minute bars after March 2024.

Rules

  • Signals use only data up to the close. Trades fill at that close and earn from the next day. Key tests were repeated with 1–10 day delays.
  • Parameters were picked on 1990–2007 and judged on 2008–2024. Spot data after March 2024 is a holdout, although the faster trend blend was chosen after seeing it.
  • Every market is sized to 15% annual volatility, so gas and crude results are comparable.
  • We ran more than 1,600 variants across all families, 1,288 of them intraday. The recommended bot has a t-statistic of 4.5 over 33 years, above the t > 3 bar suggested for new strategies.

Where the backtest can mislead

  • CFD swaps are modelled as futures roll yield plus a 2.5% markup. Your broker may differ.
  • The main WTI series holds the December contract. On near-month contracts, which are closer to what the CFDs track, the recommended bot's 2008–2024 Sharpe was 0.70 against 0.83 on the main series (0.52 against 0.59 without the crack tilt).
  • Henry Hub and Dated Brent spot can diverge sharply from the futures the CFDs follow: Henry Hub printed $30.72 in January 2026. Gas results for the last 12 months are left out for that reason.
  • Weekend gaps, broker outages and the April 2020 negative-price event are not fully captured by daily closes.
