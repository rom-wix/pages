We trained logistic regression and LightGBM models walk-forward from 2005. They were refit every year with a five-day gap between training and test data. The 50 inputs were returns over eight horizons, volatility ratios, distance from moving averages, the trend forecast, carry, the crack spread, the other markets and the calendar. Targets were next-day and next-five-day direction.

Next-day direction is noise

Out-of-sample accuracy was 49.5–52.3%, below simply predicting "up" for crude, and the probabilities were worse than the base rate. Trading those forecasts means 60–100 round trips a year, and costs turn a small edge into a loss.

The best model rediscovered carry

The five-day logistic model is trend plus the curve's carry. A two-line rule, half trend and half carry z-score, matched it exactly (0.73 either way when blended with trend). That carry signal is the fragile one from the carry section: 0.63 falls to 0.29 when traded a day late, it fails on gas, and it was flat in the 2020s.

Where ML could still help

LightGBM was worse than the linear model in every setup. Price-only features seem to be exhausted by trend and carry. A model is worth revisiting only with new information such as inventories, positioning or options data, which this sandbox couldn't reach.
