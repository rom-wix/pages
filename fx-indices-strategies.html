FX & Indices Edge Hunt

I searched for short-term strategies (15m–4H charts, 1–10 trades a week, holding minutes to days) on 8 FX pairs and 8 stock indices, and backtested them on the most recent data I could reach. No strategy passed. The details below cover what was tested, why it failed, and what would change the answer.

Verdict: nothing is ready to trade

Every standard short-term idea (breakouts, opening ranges, pullbacks, range fades, gap fades, news shocks, session and fix flows) either lost money after costs or made money in one half of the sample and lost it in the other. The handful of instrument-level "winners" is smaller than what luck alone produces in a search this size.

What was tested

Instruments: EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, AUDUSD, USDCAD, USDCHF, EURJPY, EURGBP · US500, NAS100, US30, US2000, GER40, EUSTX50, JPN225, AUS200 (UK100, NZDUSD, GBPJPY and gold were not available). Signals come from 15m, 1h and 4h bars. Every entry, stop and target is resolved on the 1-minute path.

  • Round 1: 13 families from the literature and common practice. Each has a default fixed in advance plus a small grid around it (222 sets), run on the full sample.
  • Round 2: 4 hypotheses built from Feb–May market behaviour only, then tested once on Jun–Sep.
  • Round 3: multi-day 4h strategies (channel breakout with trailing stop, index dip-buying) against a random-entry benchmark.
  • Exploratory checks that stopped at the design stage: US-index afternoon momentum, the USD "fix clock" (Krohn–Mueller–Whelan 2024), and an end-of-day momentum surface.

R = profit ÷ initial risk (entry to stop), after costs, so +0.10 means an average trade earns a tenth of what it risks. Costs: round-trip spread + commission (EURUSD 0.9 pip, USDJPY 1.0, GBPUSD 1.3 … US500 0.6 pt, NAS100 2.0, GER40 1.5), stop-fill slippage of 0.1 pip + 10% of the fill minute's range, and financing for positions held overnight.

Round 2: designed on Feb–May, tested on Jun–Sep

Feb–May diagnostics showed two structural facts. JPY pairs trend (variance ratio above 1), and most other markets mean-revert intraday. Large 15-minute moves in JPY pairs tended to continue, large moves in US indices and FX majors tended to reverse, and large 1h moves in European and Asian indices continued. Round 3: multi-day 4h trades

  • 4h channel breakout (stop order at the 10/20/40-bar high or low, 1.5 ATR initial stop, 1.5–3 ATR trailing stop, 10-day limit), all 16 instruments: −0.06 to −0.09 R per trade across the 9 settings. JPY pairs alone reached up to +0.27 R, but only from short trades, with the three best trades supplying 70–200% of the net profit.
  • Index dip-buy (4h close at a 1-day low, ≥ 1–1.5 ATR below the 5-day high, prior daily close above the 20-day midpoint; TP 0.75–1.5 ATR, SL 1–1.5 ATR, 5-day limit): best setting +0.08 R on 91 trades (t < 1). Random long entries with the same exits averaged −0.03 R, so the dip rule adds little beyond the 2026 bull market.

Why the usual strategies failed in 2026

  1. Intraday mean reversion. Most instruments reversed their multi-hour moves (variance ratio < 1 below). That is poison for breakouts, ORB and momentum-style intraday systems. The exceptions are USDJPY and EURJPY (highlighted), which trended in both halves.
  2. Costs are large relative to intraday edges. A round trip costs 0.02–0.17R depending on stop size. Most families' edge before costs was between −0.09R and +0.03R.
  3. Behaviour changes within months. Round 2 showed effects that were significant one way in Feb–May and the other way in Jun–Sep.

A data trap that looked like an edge

The first pass found a strong result: buy USDJPY and EURJPY at 07:00 Tokyo time and sell at the 09:55 fix (+0.16R per trade, t 4.1, positive in both halves). It was an artefact. The vendor's quotes are bid prices. Around the 17:00 New York rollover, spreads widen, so the bid sags for about an hour and snaps back at about 22:00 UTC, which is 07:00 Tokyo. Every pair showed the same jump in that minute, whether USD was the base or the quote currency. A real trader buys at the ask there and gets nothing. The engine now blocks entries, targets and time exits between 16:40 and 18:10 New York. Stop-losses still trigger in that window, as a real sell-stop would. All results above include this fix.

Next steps

  1. Fill in Sep 2025 – Jan 2026. Either add datafeed.dukascopy.com to the environment's allowed domains, or export 1-minute bars from MT4/MT5 (UTC timestamps) to fx-indices-research/data/raw/<SYMBOL>.csv. Then rebuild and rerun: python3 src/data.py && python3 experiments/e01_run_all.py. The split points and every rule stay fixed, so the extra months are a clean out-of-sample test.
  2. News with surprises, not just volatility. The news test here detects releases by candle size. An economic calendar with actual vs consensus (NFP, CPI, central-bank decisions) would allow surprise-direction strategies. Each event type has only 5–8 releases in this window, so it needs the longer history.
  3. Bid/ask data. The vendor supplies bids only, so short stop-losses (which trigger on the ask) are slightly optimistic. A broker export with spreads would close that gap.

Method details

  • Fills: market orders at the next minute's open; stop orders at the level or a gapped open, plus slippage; if a stop and a target are both inside one minute, the stop wins; in the entry minute of a stop/limit order only the stop is checked. A random-entry control loses −0.01R before costs from these conservative rules alone.
  • Data checks: the 1-minute snapshots agree exactly where they overlap; NFP releases from Feb to Sep appear at the right minute on EURUSD, USDJPY and US500 at once; FX cross-rate levels drift up to ~1.6% between days before late August (vendor stitching) but hold to ~1 bp within a day, which is what intraday tests need.
  • One position per strategy and instrument at a time; OCO pairs keep the first leg filled.
  • Code, trade lists and CSVs: fx-indices-research/ in this repository. This is research, not investment advice.
